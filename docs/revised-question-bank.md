Version: proposed Standard V1.1

## Learner instructions

Answer for how you work now, not for the engineer you think you should be. When possible, think of a real project example. This is a formative self-reflection, not a grade.

## Questions 29–30: Engineering Judgment

These scenarios are short cross-cutting checks. Their ordered responses create an evidence-practice indicator for interpretation only. They must not create a seventh competency or silently modify radar scores.

### 29 — J01: unexpected sensor data

**A prototype’s sensor readings suddenly become inconsistent. What would you be most likely to do first?**

1. Keep adjusting different parts until the readings look better.
2. Replace the sensor immediately because it is probably faulty.
3. Repeat the same test to see whether the issue disappears.
4. Check the setup, expected range, connections, and calibration, changing one likely cause at a time.
5. Define a short diagnostic test, compare logged evidence with expected behaviour, isolate variables, and document the conclusion before changing the design.

Interpretation: higher-order options show stronger evidence isolation and diagnostic discipline. This scenario cuts across Problem Identification, Hands-on Skills, and Design Thinking and Prototyping.

### 30 — J02: evidence challenges the preferred design

**Your team prefers its current design, but a fair test shows that it misses an important success criterion. What would you be most likely to do?**

1. Keep the design and avoid emphasising the failed result.
2. Repeat the test until the team gets a result it can present.
3. Mention the result but continue because changing direction would be inconvenient.
4. Review the test quality and criterion with the team, then revise the design or justify an explicit trade-off.
5. Verify the evidence, compare feasible alternatives and trade-offs, agree on a documented decision, and communicate the limitation clearly to stakeholders.

Interpretation: higher-order options show stronger use of evidence in planning, collaboration, iteration, and communication.

## Result interpretation notes

- A “strength” is a relatively high current profile area, not a permanent trait or assigned engineering type.
- Growth guidance should combine G01 priorities, relevant profile evidence, project context, and one feasible next action.
- C01/C02 can soften confidence language. For example, a high technical self-rating with no completed project experience should be framed as confidence to test in a project, not treated as invalid.
- J01/J02 can produce a brief reflection such as “You tend to use structured evidence when decisions become uncertain.” Do not display them as an exam grade.
- Field selection and I01 never affect competency or toolkit scores.
