# Engineering Compass Standard V1.5 — Pilot Plan

## Pilot purpose

Run a small formative pilot to check usability, wording, response flow, and whether the results are understandable and useful. This pilot is for content and experience refinement, not psychometric validation, student ranking, or research claims.

## Recommended participants

- Approximately **8–15 engineering students**.
- Include a mix of experience levels where practical, such as students with little project experience and students who have completed several projects.
- Participation should be voluntary. Do not collect unnecessary personal or sensitive information.

## Suggested session

1. Ask each participant to complete the public Standard V1.5 assessment on their usual device without coaching.
2. Observe completion time and note where a participant pauses, rereads, goes back, or asks for clarification.
3. Ask the participant to review the radar, Technical Toolkit, strengths, growth priorities, and evidence-practice reflection.
4. Collect short post-assessment feedback immediately while the experience is fresh.

Aim for a 15–20 minute session: roughly 8–10 minutes for the assessment and 5–10 minutes for feedback.

## What to observe

### Completion and comprehension

- Total completion time and any unusually slow sections.
- Questions or terms students found unclear, ambiguous, repetitive, or difficult to answer from experience.
- Whether students understand the difference between the behavioural self-description scale and the technical experience/independence scale.
- Whether the project-context and scenario choices feel easy to interpret without extra explanation.

### Result credibility and usefulness

- Whether the six-competency radar feels credible to the participant.
- Whether the nine Technical Toolkit bars are understandable as self-reported experience and independence rather than tested ability.
- Whether learner-selected growth priorities appear correctly and feel more useful than simply showing the lowest score.
- Whether the strengths and evidence-practice reflection feel fair, specific enough, and actionable.
- Whether students can name one realistic next learning or project action after reading the result.

### Response patterns

- Obvious ceiling effects, especially excessive use of 4–5 across behavioural or technical items.
- Items where almost everyone chooses the same response regardless of experience.
- Repeated use of the midpoint because students are unsure rather than genuinely intermediate.
- Any mismatch between high technical independence ratings and very limited project experience; treat this as a wording signal, not evidence of dishonesty.

### Mobile usability

- Readability and tap-target comfort on a phone.
- Whether long B13, project-context, and judgment options fit without confusing scrolling.
- Whether progress, Previous/Next actions, unrestricted multi-select interactions, and the final results remain clear on a small screen.
- Whether the radar labels and all nine toolkit names remain legible.

## Suggested post-assessment questions

Use a short anonymous form or interview notes. A five-point response plus an optional comment is sufficient where appropriate.

1. About how long did the assessment take you?
2. Which question, if any, was hardest to understand? Why?
3. Was the difference between the two response scales clear?
4. Did the six-competency radar feel credible for how you currently work? What felt right or wrong?
5. Was the Technical Toolkit profile easy to understand?
6. Did the growth guidance reflect what you actually want to improve?
7. Was any result wording confusing, judgmental, or too confident?
8. What is one action you might take after seeing your result?
9. How easy was the assessment to use on your device?
10. What is the single most important change you would make?

## Pilot notes template

For each participant, record only what is needed for refinement:

- device type;
- approximate completion time;
- unclear item IDs and the participant's explanation;
- scale confusion, if any;
- result sections that felt credible or not credible;
- mobile or interaction issues;
- one suggested improvement.

Use participant codes rather than names if notes need to be compared.

## Decision rule for Standard V1.5

After 8–15 sessions, group feedback by issue rather than by participant. Prioritise changes that:

1. recur across several students;
2. change how an item is understood;
3. create scale confusion or mobile friction; or
4. make result interpretation misleading.

Standard V1.5 should remain a wording and usability refinement unless the pilot reveals a clear structural problem. Do not begin Pro, a teacher dashboard, database collection, grouping, or course integration as part of this pilot round.
